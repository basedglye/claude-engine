## 1. BLOCKING — `SEGMENT_MIN_HOTEL_TIER` gates the quota, not the mix

`alpha-loop` is green on eight of nine assertions and red on:

> `no business-segment guest arrives before the tier-2 renovation, and at
> least one arrives after`

Measured on the tuned build, by replaying the scenario and bucketing
`guest.arrived` payloads around the `hotel.renovated {to: 2}` event:

```
arrival segments: { leisure: 32, family: 24, business: 29 }
business arrivals BEFORE tier 2: 11      AFTER: 18
```

`arrivalsForDay` correctly returns `business: 0` at tiers 0 and 1 and
correctly takes no RNG draws for it — that half is right. But
`guestSpawnSystem` picks each arriving guest's `segment` independently of
`arrivalsBySegment`, so business guests keep walking through the door of
a motel that, by the demand model, none of them booked. The vision's
sentence — *"tier 0 fills only with the cheap segment; tier 2 unlocks the
premium one"* — is therefore not implemented; only its effect on the
daily headcount is.

The fix is in `guestSpawnSystem`: draw the segment from the segments
eligible at the current `hotel.tier` (ideally weighted by that day's
`arrivalsBySegment`, which is already computed and already on the hotel
component's day rollover path). Keep the draw at generation time and keep
the sorted-key iteration, or the stream moves again.

**Do not weaken the assertion.** It is the only thing in the suite that
proves the premium segment is a reward rather than a label, and it caught
a real gap on its first run — which is the best evidence a gate can offer
for its own worth.

### What was actually wrong, and it was not only the multiplier

The reported symptom was a tier-0 cash plateau, and `TIER_DEMAND_MULT[0]
= 700` was indeed starving day one. But raising it and opening leisure at
tier 0 was **not** enough, and the reason is worth writing down because
it would have been retuned in circles otherwise:

```
day 1  rev  20000   cash  15500   forecast 6
day 2  rev  46500   cash  57500   forecast 4
       >>> RENOVATED to tier 1
day 3  rev  45100   cash  38100   forecast 0     <-- demand dies here
day 4  rev      0   cash  33600   forecast 0
...    (zero revenue for the rest of the run)
```

Demand collapsed **the day after the first renovation**, not before it.
`arrivalsForDay` prices every segment against the *cheapest room rate on
offer*, and the owner bot chases every rate up to `maxRateMinor` whenever
it has nothing else to do. Renovating raised the ceiling from $60 to
$120, the bot promptly charged $120, and a $50-willingness family and a
$60-willingness leisure guest both fell off the price term's clamp. The
hotel priced itself out of its own market as a direct reward for
renovating.

So the rate ceilings are the real control surface, and the rule that
makes them safe is now written in the file: **each tier's ceiling sits at
or just above the lowest willingness among the segments that book at that
tier.** With `[6_000, 6_500, 7_500]` a rate-maximising bot stays inside
everyone's price tolerance, and the tier reward arrives as *more guests*
(the multiplier) rather than as a higher price that drives them away.

## 3. FIX-LIST

### 3.1 BLOCKING — circular wait at the entrance cell (22,6) deadlocks the hotel

**Symptom.** 46 `nav.stuck` events; permanently frozen queue from day 8;
three-and-a-half zero-revenue days at tier 2.

**Reproduce.** `npm run harness --silent -- alpha-loop --verify-replay`,
exit 1, final hash **1503325859**, first `nav.stuck` at **tick 61791**,
entity 518, cell **(22,6)**. Agent state at tick 84000, from a
`withComponent("navAgent")` dump (entity, cell, state, target cell,
occupant of that target):

```
 459  (22,7) leaving  -> (22,6)  occupied by 821   pathLen 2   stuck 0
 463  (22,7) leaving  -> (22,6)  occupied by 821   pathLen 2   stuck 0
 467  (22,8) leaving  -> (22,7)  occupied by 463               stuck 20728
 475  (21,6) leaving   no path                                 stuck 1
 487  (22,8) leaving  -> (22,7)  occupied by 463               stuck 22099
 518  (22,6) queued   -> (22,7)  occupied by 463               stuck 19078   goal (2,10)
 522  (22,6) queued   -> (21,6)  occupied by 475               stuck 19078   goal (3,10)
 526..552 (22,6) queued, same two targets, all stuck 19078
 556..694 (22,6) arriving, pathLen 0, goal (22,6)  <- 48 bodies on the spawn cell
```

**Root cause, and it is three interlocking facts, not one.**

1. **The spawn cell and the departure goal are the same single cell.**
   Every guest spawns onto `streetCell` (22,6) and every `leaving` guest
   paths *to* (22,6) to despawn. Inbound and outbound traffic contend for
   one cell with no capacity, through a one-lane doorway at (22,7)/(21,6).
2. **A guest that cannot get a queue slot waits on top of the spawn
   cell.** The queue caps at 8; arrivals 9..48 sit at (22,6) with
   `path: []` forever — their goal *is* the cell they are standing on, so
   `findJitteredPath` returns immediately and they never move, never
   yield, and are never reaped. They become a permanent plug that
   `leaving` guests cannot path through.
3. **The H1a yield rule cannot break this cycle, by construction.** The
   lower-id-waits ordering is a total order and so cannot cycle *on its
   own* — but the cycle here closes through an agent that is not
   participating in the ordering at all. 518 (queued, lowest id in the
   queue) waits on 463, which is lower-id and therefore never yields;
   463 wants (22,6), held by 821, which is higher-id, so 463 takes the
   **sidestep** branch — and the sidestep is a **no-op here**, because
   `findJitteredPath` deliberately ignores `avoidIdx` when the avoided
   cell is the goal cell (`nIdx === avoidIdx && nIdx !== goalIdx`).
   463 clears its path and A* hands back the identical two-cell route,
   every tick, forever. Its `stuckTicks` is 0 the whole time, so
   `nav.stuck` never even fires for the agent that is actually causing
   the jam. Meanwhile 821 has no path at all and will never vacate.

   This is exactly the symmetry the yield rule exists to break, and it
   escapes it because **goal arrival is not modelled**: an agent standing
   *on its own goal* is treated as a moving agent that will eventually
   leave, when in fact it is a wall.

**Fix (shape, not a patch — the lane owns the implementation).** All
three facts need answering; fixing only the yield rule will move the
deadlock rather than remove it.

- An agent whose `path` is empty **and** whose current cell is its own
  goal must be treated as a permanent obstacle by the agent behind it:
  the yield rule's `willVacate` lookahead must return false for it
  regardless of entity id, and the blocked agent must be able to route
  around it. That means the sidestep branch must be reachable for a
  *lower*-id blocker in this specific case, or the standing agent must be
  pushed off its cell.
- `leaving` guests must despawn on **reaching the street region**, not on
  reaching one exact cell — thirteen un-reaped bodies parked in the
  entrance corridor is the mechanism that turns a delay into a permanent
  jam.
- Overflow arrivals must not stack on the spawn cell. Either they wait on
  distinct street cells, or they are turned away with a `guest.left` —
  a motel that is genuinely full is a legible outcome; an invisible pile
  of 48 people in a doorway is not.

## 2. The three round-3 facts, judged

### Fact 3 — `stuckTicks` on a self-arrival: SOUND

`findJitteredPath` returns a path whose `path[0]` is the agent's own
current cell, so the first `moveSystem` pass after every repath hits
`targetIdx === curIdx` and used to reset `stuckTicks` to 0 for free. The
sidestep branch clears the path, which forces a repath, which bought a
free reset — so an agent could sidestep-and-reset forever and never trip
its own tripwire. That is a correct root cause and `isSelfArrival ?
agent.stuckTicks : 0` is the minimal correct answer to it.

It does not weaken the tripwire in the other direction: any arrival at a
*real* target still zeroes the counter, and a real target is reached on
every tick an agent actually moves, so `stuckTicks` still measures
"consecutive ticks of zero real progress" and nothing else. No agent that
is making progress can accumulate toward `nav.stuck`.

**Determinism is intact.** `isSelfArrival` is a pure function of the
agent's own `path`/`pathIdx`/`pos`, all of them write-through state; it
reads nothing from another agent and nothing order-dependent. The H1a
yield rule's stable-id priority is untouched — agents are still iterated
ascending by `EntityId`, the jitter seed is still per-agent and still
consumed by `findJitteredPath` identically, and no `Rng` is touched
anywhere in this diff. The twelve identical hashes are the empirical
confirmation.

### Fact 1 — the parked-occupant rule: SOUND, with one hole it does not close

The rule is right and its justification is right: an occupant with no
queued step, standing on its own goal, is skipped entirely at the top of
`moveSystem` (`if (agent.pathIdx >= agent.path.length) continue`), so it
is a wall, not a traveller, and giving it an unconditional lower-id
"wait" was the bug. Forcing `willVacate = false` and making
`sidestepEligible` true for it regardless of id is the correct shape.

**No new livelock between two parked agents.** A parked agent never
reaches the yield code at all (same early `continue`), so it can never be
the *blocked* party. Two parked agents cannot form a cycle; a parked
agent and a mover cannot either, because only the mover ever waits.

**A parked agent at a doorway does not livelock either, but only because
`pathSystem` falls back:** the sidestep records `avoidCx/avoidCz`,
`pathSystem` tries the detour first and falls back to the un-avoided path
when no detour exists, so a single-lane doorway degrades to waiting
rather than to path loss. Correct, and already commented.

**The hole.** `findJitteredPath` ignores `avoidIdx` when the avoided cell
**is the goal cell** (`nIdx === avoidIdx && nIdx !== goalIdx`). So when a
parked agent is parked on the blocked agent's *own goal*, the sidestep is
a guaranteed no-op: identical path, re-block, forever. Round 4 does not
change that line and does not need to — but it means the fix's safety
depends entirely on **no two agents ever being given the same goal cell**,
which is precisely what item 4.1 below violates.

### Fact 2 — region despawn: HONEST

`reachedStreetRegion` is reachable only from `cleanupSystem`, and only
for guests already in state `leaving` and candidates already in state
`rejected`. Both of those states set their goal to `streetCell` at the
transition (`guestBrainSystem` lines 1146/1170, `applyStaffDecision`),
so the region is the agent's own destination in every case — there is no
agent for whom (22,6) is merely *en route*. A guest "passing by" cannot
be swept, because a guest with any other business is not in `leaving`.

The `stuckTicks >= STUCK_THRESHOLD` gate is the right second lock and is
the reason I am comfortable with a 2-cell Chebyshev radius: proximity

**Economically neutral, which is the part that mattered most.** Revenue,

### 4.1 BLOCKING — the overflow wait pool re-creates "two agents, one goal cell"

`overflowWaitCellFor(entity) = overflowWaitCells[entity % overflowWaitCells.length]`.
Two simultaneously-overflowing guests whose ids differ by exactly the pool
size are handed **the same goal cell**. The first to arrive parks on it
(empty path, standing on its own goal); the second is then permanently
blocked by a parked agent sitting on its own goal — the one case where,
per §2 fact 1 above, the sidestep is a guaranteed no-op because
`findJitteredPath` ignores `avoidIdx` at the goal. The second guest never
moves, never yields, accumulates `stuckTicks` (now that fact 3 no longer
masks it), fires `nav.stuck`, and becomes a fresh permanent plug.

This is not a hypothetical class of bug: **the code twenty lines above
the new pool already says so**, about the candidate flow —

> *"One wait cell PER candidate in a round: two candidates sharing a goal
> cell means the second yields against the first forever and never reaches
> 'waiting' at all (observed, before this)."*

The overflow path reintroduces exactly that, keyed by modulo instead of by
allocation. It did not fire on seed `hotel-alpha-loop-1` because the pool
is large relative to the concurrent overflow; that is luck, not a
property, and it is seed-dependent in a project whose whole contract is
that a seed reproduces.

**Fix.** Make distinctness an invariant, not an accident: allocate wait
cells by **rank among the currently-overflowing guests** (the loop already
iterates `arriving` sorted ascending by id — a counter incremented per
overflow guest is deterministic and needs no `Rng`), and turn away
immediately, on the same `guest.left` path, once the rank exceeds the pool
size. Assert it: a check that no two live agents share a `(goalCx, goalCz)`
among the overflow pool.

cells at distance 0 from `streetCell` and the queue cells (`distSq < 1`),
so the pool's *nearest* members are (22,7) and (21,6) — the one-lane
