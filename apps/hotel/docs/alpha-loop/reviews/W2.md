## Fix-list

### 1. BLOCKING — the neon VACANCY sign renders as a solid black rectangle

**Visible in: `W2-shots/t0-facade.png`** — dead centre, above the door.

`exterior.ts` builds the housing `signBox` (depth 0.15) centred at
`doorZ - 0.4`, so it spans `doorZ - 0.475 … doorZ - 0.325`, and then puts
`neonFace` at `doorZ - 0.325`. The camera is on the street at *smaller*
z, so the housing's front face (`doorZ - 0.475`) sits **in front of** the
neon plane and occludes it completely. On top of that, a `PlaneGeometry`
faces +z by default, i.e. away from the street, so even unoccluded it
would be back-facing to a player walking up to the motel. Two mistakes
pointing the same way — exactly the signature of geometry authored
without ever being looked at.

**Corroborated from a second angle.** While reviewing W4 I photographed
the artifact build from inside the lobby and the neon reads clearly as
pink lettering through the entrance glass
(`../reviews/W4-shots/artifact-sweep.png`, upper left). The texture, the emissive
material and the glow all work: the sign is simply mounted facing the
lobby with its housing between it and the street.

The VACANCY sign is named in the vision's tier-0 table and is the one
element that says "motel" from across the street. Right now the player's
first impression of the game is a black slab on a pole, which also sits
directly in front of the entrance door.

Move the face to the street side of the housing (`doorZ - 0.476` or
nearer) and orient it toward the street, then **re-shoot `t0-facade` and
confirm the word VACANCY is legible in the PNG.** `neonSignTexture`
itself is fine — it is only ever mounted wrong.

### 2. BLOCKING — the drop-ceiling grid is gold

**Visible in: `t0-lobby-east.png`, `t0-desk.png`, `t0-corridor.png`,
`t0-bedroom.png`** — every tier-0 interior pose, dominating each frame.

`architecture.ts`'s tier-0 branch accumulates the ceiling tee bars into
`accFor("metal-brass")`. That is the *brass* material: the tees render as
bright polished gold. A suspended-ceiling tee is white or dull
aluminium, and gilding the grid makes the cheapest surface in the
building read as the most expensive one — which inverts the entire point
of the tier. In `t0-bedroom.png` the gold grid is the brightest thing in
frame.

Give the tees their own dull off-white / aluminium material (high
roughness, low metalness). No new texture needed.

### 3. Tier 1 is not "clean carpet", it is differently-stained carpet

**Visible in: `t1-lobby-east.png`** (blobs on the floor by the sofa) and
**`t1-facade.png`** (blobs on the entrance floor, seen through the door).

`proceduralMatFor` calls `stainedCarpetTexture(hotelTier === 0 ? 1 : 2)`,
so tier 1 gets the same stain generator with seed 2 and the stains simply
move. The vision's tier-1 row says **"clean carpet"**, and the player's
reward for spending $750 is currently that the stains are in different
places. Either add a `clean` flag that skips the stain pass, or give tier
1 its own generator. Re-shoot `t1-lobby-east` to confirm.

### 4. Non-blocking — tier 0 reads "cheap office", not "seedy motel"

**Visible in: all four tier-0 interior poses.**

The materials and props are right and legible — fluorescent troughs,
folding chair, vending machine, plastic ficus, laminate counter,
mismatched bedroom furniture, chain-link and dumpster outside. What is
missing is *tone*: the tier-0 interiors are bright, evenly lit and
clean-walled. `lighting.ts` does drop the key to 0.65 and shift the
hemisphere green, but at these poses the result still reads as
well-maintained. The lobby flicker is wired onto the tier-0 trough, which
single frames cannot judge.

Suggestion rather than prescription: push tier 0's ambient and hemisphere
down further so the troughs are the only bright thing, and dirty
`scuffedPaintTexture` around the wainscot line. This is taste, so it does
not block — but the vision's word is "tired", and a re-shoot should make
me feel it.

### 5. Non-blocking — `decor.ts` carries a module-level `activeTier`

`buildDecor` sets a file-scope `let activeTier` that every helper and the
async `upgrade()` closure read. The material cache *is* correctly keyed by
tier, and `upgrade()` captures `tierAtSchedule` to guard the async path,
so this is defensible and I am not blocking on it. But it makes
`buildDecor` non-reentrant by construction: the moment anything builds two
tiers in one frame, or a glTF resolves across a tier swap in an order
nobody predicted, the wrong material lands and it will present as a
one-off rendering glitch that nobody can reproduce. Prefer threading the
tier through as an argument next time the file is open.

### 1 (was BLOCKING) — neon VACANCY: FIXED

`coo-t0-facade.png`: the word **VACANCY** reads in pink neon on a black
housing, dead centre above the door, from a street camera I chose myself
and at a distance a player would first see it from. `exterior.ts` now puts
`neonFace` at `doorZ - 0.476` — the street side of the housing, which spans
`doorZ - 0.475 … doorZ - 0.325` — and applies `rotation.y = Math.PI` so the
plane faces `-Z`. Both halves of the round-1 defect (occlusion and
back-facing) are addressed, and the comment in the source states the
geometry reasoning, so the next person to move it will know why it is
where it is. This was the vision's one "reads as a motel from across the
street" element; it now does.

Recorded, not charged: the rusty marquee pole still runs straight down the
middle of the entrance doorway (visible in both my facade frame and the
lane's). It was in the round-1 frame too and I did not list it, so I am not
listing it now — but it will look like a mistake to a playtester, and
shifting the pole a metre off-centre is a one-line change whenever
`exterior.ts` is next open.

### 2 (was BLOCKING) — the ceiling grid: FIXED

`architecture.ts` gives the tees their own `"ceiling-tee"` material
(`0xc7c6c0`, roughness 0.85, metalness 0.15) built in `proceduralMatFor`
and accumulated via `accFor("ceiling-tee")` — no longer `metal-brass`. In
`coo-t0-corridor.png`, `coo-t0-lobby-east.png`, `coo-t0-desk.png` and
`coo-t0-bedroom.png` the grid is dull off-white aluminium against the
grey-green tile, exactly what a suspended ceiling looks like. Against the
round-1 frame at the same corridor pose, the change is unmistakable: the
brightest object in the frame is now the fluorescent trough, which is the
correct answer. The cheapest surface in the building no longer reads as
the most expensive one.

### 3 (was open) — tier 1 is actually clean carpet: FIXED

`proceduralMatFor` now calls `cleanCarpetTexture()` for tier 1 instead of
re-running the stain generator with a different seed — no stain pass at
all. The lane's own two tier-1 frames happen not to show much floor, so I
shot `coo-t1-corridor.png` myself specifically to see it: a flat, even
biscuit carpet with no blobs, under a chair rail and a warm can light. Put
beside `coo-t0-corridor.png` (visibly stained) the $750 now buys something
a player can see.
